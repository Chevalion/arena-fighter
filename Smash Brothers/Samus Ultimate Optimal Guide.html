Samus Ultimate Optimal Guide

Interactive reference for optimal Samus play in Super Smash Bros. Ultimate. Strategy notes for pressure, bombs, tech chases, and recovery.

Core Identity

Samus is a keep-away / zoning character whose primary tool is the threat of Charge Shot. The charge itself creates pressure before it is ever fired. Opponents must respect the possibility of a full charge, which forces them to approach or jump in ways you can punish.

Strengths
  • Charge Shot controls space and deals heavy damage / kills at higher percents.
  • Excellent projectile suite (missiles, bombs, charge) for stage control and conditioning.
  • Strong edgeguarding and ledge trapping.
  • Good grab game with a tether that has range.
  • Bomb setups create delayed threats that force decisions.
Weaknesses
  • Linear recovery (Screw Attack is predictable; tether can be challenged).
  • Slow ground speed; loses if an opponent gets in and stays in.
  • Charge Shot is reactable if held indefinitely without mixing.
  • Limited close-range buttons.

Key Moves to Use Most

  • Charge Shot (Neutral B) — Core of the gameplan. Hold to threaten, release full or partial. Mix uncharged shots and cancels.
  • Homing Missile — Tracks the opponent. Tilt up for high arc, down for low skim. Forces jumps and approaches.
  • Super Missile (Side B) — Straight-line, fast, high damage. Can be charged. Kill tool at higher percents when unexpected.
  • Bombs (Down B) — Stage control, tech chase coverage, ledge traps, recovery mix-ups. Detonation timing is controllable.
  • Screw Attack (Up B) — Up-special with multi-hitbox and tether recovery. Use to surprise or recover; do not default to it.
  • Fair / Bair — Spacing and air presence when opponents get closer.
  • Grab / Zair — Grounded grab for throws and tech chases. Aerial grab (zair) is a ranged poke with low landing lag.
  • Down Tilt — Fast low poke that pairs with bomb setups.

Charge Shot Pressure

Always having charge on is correct as a baseline, but predictability is the risk. Mix full charges, partial shots, dash cancels, and jump cancels. The goal is to make every approach expensive. The charge is the threat; the other tools convert the fear into damage.

Mix options
  • Full charge releases.
  • Partial / uncharged shots.
  • Dash cancels and jump cancels out of charge.
  • Immediate follow-ups (missile, bomb, grab) after conditioning a jump or shield.

Missile Usage

Homing Missile — Neutral B (or after holding charge and releasing with direction). Tracks. Use to force movement.
Super Missile — Side B. Straight, faster, harder-hitting. Charge it for extra damage. Best as a surprise kill option or to punish a committed approach.

Angle both with the stick: up for high trajectory, down for low.

Screw Attack

Up B. Spins with hitboxes and can tether to ledges. It is both an attack and a recovery tool. It is linear, so good opponents will anti-air or edgeguard it. Use it to surprise or when you need the tether, not as a default escape.

Bomb Fundamentals

Bombs spawn on frame 11. They auto-explode on frame 83 if left alone. Holding down detonates earlier (around frame 44 on ground, 48 in air). Explosion has a radius (~5 units) centered on the bomb.

83 frames (long)

Click the button to watch a visual of the long timer. Hold-down mode is roughly half the length.

Detonation control
  • Release Down B → long timer (frame 83).
  • Hold Down → shorter timer (frame 44-ish).
  • This lets you time the explosion to an opponent's landing or tech.

You can have multiple active bombs. Dropping two side-by-side creates a wider coverage wall.

Bomb Tech Chase & Wall

The bomb does not track. Place it so the explosion radius covers both left and right escape routes.

Basic placement
  1. Drop bomb directly under or on the opponent's expected landing/tech spot.
  2. The blast covers both directions from the center.
  3. Hold or release to time the detonation to their tech window.
Wall technique (two bombs)
  1. Drop one bomb.
  2. Immediately drop a second bomb offset to one side.
  3. The combined radius covers a wider area, punishing rolls in either direction.
  4. Use when you have time (after a knockdown or at ledge).
Consistent trapping
  • After knocking them down, drop bomb(s) on the tech spot.
  • Walk forward slightly and threaten with down tilt or grab.
  • If they roll toward the bomb, the explosion catches them.
  • If they roll away, chase or cover with another bomb / projectile.
  • Mix the punishment so they cannot call the pattern.
Frame trap idea
  • Drop bomb with long timer.
  • Follow with a fast move (down tilt, jab, short hop nair).
  • Opponent must respect both the immediate move and the upcoming explosion.
  • Holding down shortens the timer for tighter timing against falling opponents.

Ledge Trapping with Bombs

  • Drop bomb on or near the ledge where the opponent will grab or stand up.
  • The explosion can hit them as they get up or roll from ledge.
  • Follow with down tilt to catch the option that avoids the bomb.
  • Give a little space so you can react to their getup (roll, jump, attack, neutral getup).
  • If they jump, you are already positioned to anti-air or punish the landing.

Bomb Jump / Bomb Roll Recovery

Offstage, dropping a bomb under yourself and detonating it can launch you upward. This is a classic Samus recovery mix-up across Smash titles.

Execution steps
  1. While falling offstage, press Down B to drop a bomb beneath you.
  2. Time the detonation (hold down for faster pop, or let it tick) so the explosion hits you from below.
  3. The upward knockback gives extra height.
  4. Combine with Screw Attack or tether afterward to reach the ledge.

It does not fully reset jumps in the same way as some other characters' tools, but the vertical boost is real and makes the recovery less linear. Practice the timing so the bomb pops when you are positioned above it.

Grab and Throw Notes

  • Grounded grab has range due to the Grapple Beam.
  • After a grab, down throw can set up tech chases or aerial follow-ups at lower percents.
  • Up throw and back throw are more for positioning or kills at higher percents.
  • Aerial grab (press grab in air) is a zair: it deals damage, has low landing lag, and is a safe poke while descending. It is not a true grab.

General Pressure Loop

  1. Hold Charge Shot to threaten.
  2. Use missiles and bombs to force approaches or jumps.
  3. Punish the forced movement with charge release, grab, or aerial.
  4. On knockdown, set bomb(s) for tech chase coverage.
  5. At ledge, layer bomb + down tilt.
  6. Mix the responses so patterns are not free to call.
